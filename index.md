## 🎶 Music Theory Analysis & Comments

Here is an analysis of the shifts:

### 1. C Maj $\rightarrow$ [Eb Maj $\rightarrow$ C min]

* **C Major** is your starting **Tonic** (I).
* **Eb Major** is the **Mediant of C minor** ($\text{III}$ of $\text{Cmin}$), but also the **bIII** (flatted-mediant) chord/key when compared to $\text{C Maj}$.
* **C minor** is the **Parallel Minor** of $\text{C Maj}$.
    * **Comment:** The shift from $\text{C Maj}$ to $\text{Eb Maj}$ is a common "pop lift" using the $\text{bIII}$ key/chord, which often feels surprising and rich. The following shift to the **parallel minor** ($\text{Cmin}$) then feels like a return to a familiar tonic root, but with a sudden darkening of mood. $\text{Eb Maj}$ is the relative major of $\text{Cmin}$, so the move $\text{Eb Maj} \rightarrow \text{Cmin}$ is a very smooth, relative key change.

### 2. C min $\rightarrow$ [Ab Maj $\rightarrow$ F Maj]

* **Ab Major** is the **Submediant of C minor** ($\text{VI}$ of $\text{Cmin}$) or the **bVI** (flatted-submediant) chord/key when compared to $\text{C Maj}$. This is a very common chord in the minor and parallel minor sound.
* **F Major** is the **Subdominant of C major** ($\text{IV}$ of $\text{C Maj}$) but *not* a diatonic key in $\text{Cmin}$ (F minor is).
    * **Comment:** The move from $\text{Cmin}$ to $\text{Ab Maj}$ is a classic minor key chord progression. The final move to **F Major** acts like a **Picardy Third** effect on the $\text{fmin}$ chord of $\text{Cmin}$, or more simply, a shift back to a "brighter" chord ($\text{IV}$ of the original $\text{C Maj}$) to set up the next section. This move from a darker key to the $\text{IV}$ of the original tonic often provides a strong pull or feeling of resolution, perhaps setting up a return to the $\text{C Maj}$ section.
